F1Critical

Self-harm question, no crisis help

What we saw

Step 20 asks: “Thought that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself in some way.” It is required.Proof E19

Why it matters

A person in crisis may answer “nearly every day” and see no help at that moment. This is the most serious item in the report.

What we cannot see. We did not fill out or send the form. Your team may get an alert after someone sends it. Only you know.

Security and exposure

What may be wrong behind the scenes

Two findings look like work by someone else. Please find out who made these changes.

How we checked. We only read public information, such as page code, public records and DNS. We did not try to break in or test logins. Active testing needs Josh’s written permission.

F3Critical

Hidden links to another company

What we saw

597 of 599 blog pages have four links to getuniqcli.com, such as “Uniqcli Mission Critical IT Procurement.”Proof E15

The code hides them: “display:none … visibility:hidden … left:-9999px.”Proof E15

Why it matters

People cannot see them. Search engines can. Hidden links break Google’s rules and can lower your ranking. They can also mean someone changed your site without telling you.

A lead. Your site has two plugins that can change code and text in bulk: Code Snippets and String Locator. Look there first for a snippet that adds this block. We cannot see inside your admin area.

What to do

Ask who manages your site and your search work. Find what adds the links and remove it. Check who has admin access and which plugins changed lately. Look in Google Search Console for warnings.

F4Critical

“Mailbox” addresses and a lookalike email domain

What we saw

“Buzón Corporativo De Compassion Recovery Centers Santa Rosa. 1264 Mendocino Ave … (714) 909-2201 … backlinks9@compassionrecoverycenters.info”Proof E16

28 city pages (16 Spanish, 12 English) list mailbox addresses. Page data holds 16 different addresses and seven emails at the .info name.Proof E16

The .info name resolves on the internet. It points to two server addresses.Proof E20

Why it matters

A mailbox is not an office. Listing mailboxes as places can look like fake locations. A lookalike domain can also be used to send fake emails that look like yours.

We do not know who owns the .info name. Our lookup was blocked. Check your registrar account.

What to do

Find out who added these. Remove any address that is not a real site you run. If you own the .info name, set up email protection on it. If you do not, ask who does.

F5High

Email protection has two conflicting records

What we saw

“v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; … rua=mailto:dmarc_rua@onsecureserver.net”Proof E20

“v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:your-email@compassionrecoverycenters.com”Proof E20

Your sender list ends in “-all,” which is strict. It names GoDaddy, SendGrid and Microsoft 365.Proof E20

Why it matters

DMARC tells email systems what to do with fake emails that pretend to be you. The rules allow only one record. With two, receivers may ignore both. The second one is an unfinished template. Fake emails that look like they come from a treatment center can hurt patients.

What to do

Keep one record. Use a real address for reports. Check that every service that sends your email is on the sender list.

The plan

Fix the Foundation First

Safety and trust come first. Then simple tools can answer people faster and cut repeat work.

Days 1 to 30

Fix the foundation

  • The ten items above
  • Two-step login for every admin
  • Safety settings at your host
  • Descriptions for the blog, and a clean sitemap

Build the Engine

Each idea helps a patient get an answer sooner, saves your team from a repeat task, or both.

Safety rule. A person handles anything about safety. If someone is in crisis, a tool must send them to 988 or a live person right away. Tools never give medical advice. Health privacy laws such as HIPAA apply, and addiction records have an extra rule, 42 CFR Part 2. Every tool that touches patient details needs a signed agreement and a check by your compliance adviser. We are not lawyers.

Days 31 to 60

Stop losing calls

  • Text back every missed call within a minute. Your call-tracking tool may already do this. Ask them.
  • One phone plan for every button. Track the two phone buttons that are not tracked today.
  • A homepage FAQ and a chat helper. It uses only answers you approve and hands off to a person.

Days 61 to 90

Save staff time

  • A short insurance form with an automatic check. People share less private data up front. Say a check is not a promise of payment.
  • Online booking with text reminders. Fewer phone calls back and forth, and fewer missed visits.
  • Paperwork finished at home. Less typing and fewer mistakes.
  • A monthly website check by Elliyeen. It reads every page and your listings, then sends a short list. It also warns you before a certificate runs out.
  • One weekly report. Calls, texts, forms and booked visits, by source.

How you will know it works

We have no call or revenue numbers yet, so we are not guessing at savings. Count these for 30 days before and after each change. Then the savings are your own numbers.
